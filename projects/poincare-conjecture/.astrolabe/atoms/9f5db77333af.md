The statement that $\triangle f\leq \frac{n-1}{f}$ in *the sense
of distributions* (or equivalently *in the weak sense*) means
that for any non-negative test function $\phi$, that is to say for
any compactly supported $C^\infty$-function $\phi$, we have

$$
\int_Mf\triangle \phi d\mathit{vol}\le \int_M\left(\frac{n-1}{f}\right)\phi d\mathit{vol}.
$$

Since the triangle inequality implies that $|f(x)-f(y)|\le d(x,y)$, it follows
that $f$ is Lipschitz, and hence that the restriction of $\nabla f$ to any
compact subset of $M$ is an $L^2$ one-form. Integration by parts then shows
that

$$
\int_Mf\triangle
\phi d\mathit{vol}=-\int_M\langle \nabla f,\nabla\phi\rangle d\mathit{vol}.
$$
