The *Ricci curvature tensor*,
denoted $\mathit{Ric}$ or $\mathit{Ric}_g$ when it is necessary to specify
the metric, is a symmetric contravariant two-tensor. In local
coordinates it is defined by

$$
\mathit{Ric}(X,Y)=g^{kl}R(X,\partial_k,Y,\partial_l).
$$

The value of this tensor at a point $p\in M$ is given by
$\sum_{i=1}^nR(X(p),e_i,Y(p),e_i)$ where $\{e_{1},\cdots,e_{n}\}$ is
an orthonormal basis of $T_pM$. Clearly $\mathit{Ric}$ is a symmetric
bilinear form on $TM$, given in local coordinates by

$$
\mathit{Ric}=\mathit{Ric}_{ij}dx^i\otimes dx^j,
$$

 where
$\mathit{Ric}_{ij}=\mathit{Ric}(\partial_i,\partial _j)$. The
*scalar curvature* is defined by:

$$
R=R_g=\mathit{tr}_g \mathit{Ric}=g^{ij}\mathit{Ric}_{ij}.
$$

 We will say that $\mathit{Ric} \geq
k$ (or $\leq k$) if all the eigenvalues of $\mathit{Ric}$ are $\geq k$
(or $\leq k$).
