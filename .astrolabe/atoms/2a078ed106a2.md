title: Sphere Theorem
---
Let $M^n$ be a compact simply connected Riemannian manifold, whose sectional
curvature $K$ satisfies

$$
0<hK_{\max}<K\leq K_{\max}.\qquad(1)
$$

Then if $h=1/4$, $M$ is homeomorphic to a sphere.

The number $h$ is called the *pinching* of $M$. Multiplying the metric by a
constant, we can suppose that $K_{\max}=1$, and (1) can be written, without loss
of generality, as

$$
0<h<K\leq 1.\qquad(1')
$$

The Sphere Theorem was proved for the first time by Rauch [R 1] for
$h\sim 3/4$. A fundamental contribution was made by Klingenberg [Kℓ 1], who
introduced into the problem the consideration of the "cut locus" (see Section 2).
For $M$ of even dimension Klingenberg obtained in [Kℓ 1] an estimate for the
distance from a point to its cut locus and proved the theorem for $h\sim 0.55$.
Using Toponogov's theorem and that estimate, Berger [Br 1] obtained the theorem,
still in even dimension, with $h=1/4$; the use of Toponogov's theorem is
unnecessary, as shown by Tsukamoto [Ts]. Finally, Klingenberg [Kℓ 2] extended his
estimate from even to odd dimension, which together with the work of Berger
[Br 1] yielded the theorem as stated.

In even dimension, the theorem is false if (1') is replaced by

$$
0<1/4\leq K\leq 1.\qquad(2)
$$

The complex projective space $P^n(\mathbb{C})$, $n>1$, is simply connected, has a
metric whose sectional curvature satisfies (2) and is not homeomorphic to a
sphere (Exercise 12, Chap. 8). Berger [Br 1] proved that if (2) holds then either
$\operatorname{diam}(M)>\pi$ and $M$ is homeomorphic to a sphere, or
$\operatorname{diam}(M)=\pi$ and $M$ is isometric to a symmetric space. In the odd
dimensional case the theorem is still true substituting (1') by (2), but it is not
known whether this can be improved. For dimensions two and three the theorem holds
supposing only $h\geq 0$: a compact simply connected $M^n$, $n=2,3$, with positive
sectional curvature is homeomorphic to $S^n$ (for $n=2$ by Gauss–Bonnet, for
$n=3$ by a theorem of Hamilton [H]).

This chapter presents the Sphere Theorem without Toponogov's theorem. Section 2
introduces the "cut locus"; Section 3 estimates the distance from a point to its
cut locus (using elements of Morse Theory, omissible in even dimension); Section 4
proves the theorem; Section 5 mentions further developments. Unless otherwise
stated, the manifolds are complete and the geodesics normalized.
