title: sectional curvature independent of basis
---
Let $\sigma\subset T_pM$ be a two-dimensional subspace of the tangent space
$T_pM$ and let $x,y\in\sigma$ be two linearly independent vectors. Then

$$
K(x,y)=\frac{(x,y,x,y)}{|x\wedge y|^2}
$$

does not depend on the choice of the vectors $x,y\in\sigma$.

*Proof.* To avoid calculating, we observe that we can pass from the basis $\{x,y\}$
of $\sigma$ to any other basis $\{x',y'\}$ by iterating the following elementary
transformations:
- (a) $\{x,y\}\to\{y,x\}$,
- (b) $\{x,y\}\to\{\lambda x,y\}$,
- (c) $\{x,y\}\to\{x+\lambda y,y\}$.

It is easy to see that $K(x,y)$ is invariant by such transformations, which
completes the proof. $\blacksquare$
