title: Christoffel symbols
---
The connection given by \entryref{2957f2c4024c} will be referred to, from now on, as the
*Levi-Civita* (or *Riemannian*) *connection* on $M$. The functions $\Gamma_{ij}^k$
defined on $U$ by $\nabla_{X_i}X_j=\sum_k\Gamma_{ij}^k X_k$ are called the
*coefficients of the connection* $\nabla$ on $U$, or the *Christoffel symbols*. From
(9) it follows that

$$
\sum_\ell\Gamma_{ij}^\ell g_{\ell k}=\tfrac{1}{2}\Big\{\frac{\partial}{\partial x_i}g_{jk}+\frac{\partial}{\partial x_j}g_{ki}-\frac{\partial}{\partial x_k}g_{ij}\Big\},
$$

where $g_{ij}=\langle X_i,X_j\rangle$. Since $(g_{km})$ admits an inverse
$(g^{km})$,

$$
\Gamma_{ij}^m=\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_k\Big\{\frac{\partial}{\partial x_i}g_{jk}+\frac{\partial}{\partial x_j}g_{ki}-\frac{\partial}{\partial x_k}g_{ij}\Big\}g^{km}.\qquad(10)
$$

Equation (10) is the classical expression for the Christoffel symbols in terms of
the $g_{ij}$. For Euclidean space $\mathbb{R}^n$ one has $\Gamma_{ij}^k=0$. In
terms of the Christoffel symbols, the covariant derivative has the classical
expression

$$
\frac{DV}{dt}=\sum_k\Big\{\frac{dv^k}{dt}+\sum_{i,j}\Gamma_{ij}^k v^j\frac{dx_i}{dt}\Big\}X_k,
$$

which follows from (1). In Euclidean spaces the covariant derivative coincides
with the usual derivative.
