## §2 The cut locus

Let $M$ be complete, $p\in M$, and $\gamma:[0,\infty)\to M$ a normalized geodesic
with $\gamma(0)=p$. For small $t>0$, $d(\gamma(0),\gamma(t))=t$, that is,
$\gamma([0,t])$ is minimizing; and if $\gamma([0,t_1])$ is not minimizing the same
holds for all $t>t_1$. By continuity, the set of $t>0$ with
$d(\gamma(0),\gamma(t))=t$ is of the form $[0,t_0]$ or $[0,\infty)$. In the first
case $\gamma(t_0)$ is called the *cut point of $p$ along $\gamma$*; in the second
no cut point exists. The *cut locus of $p$*, denoted $C_m(p)$, is the union of the
cut points of $p$ along all geodesics starting from $p$. If $M$ is compact, a cut
point exists along every geodesic from $p$; the converse holds (Cor. 2.11).

### 2.6′ Remark (wiedersehen manifold)
A compact Riemannian manifold $M$ for which the cut locus $C_m(p)$ of every
$p\in M$ reduces to a single point is called a *wiedersehen manifold*. By a theorem
of L. Green [Gn], wiedersehen surfaces ($\dim M=2$) are isometric to the sphere;
this was generalized to even $\dim M$ by Berger and Kazdan [Be, pp. 236–246] and to
odd $\dim M$ by C. T. Yang [Yg]. See Kazdan [Ka].

## §3 The estimate of the injectivity radius

The next proposition is a crucial step in the Sphere Theorem; its proof uses Morse
Theory. A reader who wishes only the even-dimensional Sphere Theorem can omit it and
pass to \entryref{096001bed96a}.

\entryblock{615bcaf45bdc}

\entryblock{d11a88e897eb}

For the next lemma we need the following facts. Let $f:M^n\to\mathbb{R}$ be a
differentiable function on a differentiable manifold $M$. A point $p\in M$ is a
critical point of $f$ if $df(p)=0$; $f(p)$ is then called a critical value of $f$.
If $p$ is a critical point and $(x_1,\dots,x_n)$ is a coordinate system on $M$
around $p$, the hessian of $f$, defined by the matrix
$(\partial^2f/\partial x_i\partial x_j)(p)$, represents a symmetric bilinear form
on $T_pM$ that does not depend on the chosen system of coordinates. We say that
the critical point is non-degenerate if the determinant of this matrix is
different from zero. Such a critical point is isolated, and it is possible to
choose a coordinate system $(x_1,\dots,x_{n-\lambda},y_1,\dots,y_\lambda)$ in such
a way that, in the coordinate neighborhood considered,

$$
f(x_1,\dots,x_{n-\lambda},y_1,\dots,y_\lambda)
=f(p)+x_1^2+\cdots+x_{n-\lambda}^2-y_1^2-\cdots-y_\lambda^2.
$$

The integer $\lambda$ is called the index of the non-degenerate critical point
$p$. (For more details see Milnor [Mi].)

## §5 Some further developments

Survey section; no numbered statements. The Sphere Theorem has produced an intense
and continuing research activity. The main lines of development mentioned by do
Carmo are:

- **Homeomorphism vs. diffeomorphism.** The homeomorphism here glues two discs along
  their boundaries, which may produce a differentiable structure on $M$ distinct from
  the standard sphere; so this proof does not give a diffeomorphism. In 1966 Gromoll
  [Gℓ] and Calabi (unpublished) proved a *differentiable* sphere theorem for a
  pinching $h_n$ sufficiently small (depending on $n$); $h_n$ has since been improved
  ([Sh], [SSK]), the best estimate at the time being $\lim h_n\sim0.68$ as
  $n\to\infty$ [IHR].

- **The case $h<1/4$ in even dimension.** Following Rauch [R2], one compares $M$ with
  a symmetric space (compact, simply connected, rank one); after results of do Carmo
  [dC 1], Klingenberg [Kl 3], Kobayashi [Ko], Cheeger [Cr 1], a satisfactory theorem
  was obtained by Min-Oo and Ruh [MR]. Berger [Br 3] showed that for $h=(1/4)-\varepsilon$
  a compact simply connected $h$-pinched manifold is still homeomorphic to a sphere or
  one of those symmetric spaces (no estimate for $\varepsilon$ is known).

- **The odd-dimensional case $h<1/4$** appears difficult: the estimate $i(M)\geq\pi$
  (valid in even dimension for any $h$ by Prop. 3.4) cannot hold in odd dimension
  without restriction on $h$. By an example of Berger (Cheeger–Ebin [CE] pp. 70–71)
  the best one can hope for is $h=1/9$, not yet proved.

- **Diameter sphere theorem (Grove–Shiohama [GS], 1977).** If $M$ is compact,
  $K\geq1$, and $\operatorname{diam}(M)>\pi/2$, then $M$ is homeomorphic to a sphere.
  The case $\operatorname{diam}(M)=\pi/2$ (where it fails, e.g. real projective space)
  was essentially classified by Gromoll and Grove [Gℓ-G].

- **Almost-constant negative curvature (Gromov [Gv 0], 1978).** If
  $-1-\varepsilon\leq K\leq-1$ and the diameter is bounded above by $D$, then for
  $\varepsilon=\varepsilon(n,D)$ small, $M$ admits a metric of constant curvature
  $-1$.

- **Betti number bound (Gromov [Gv 2]).** For compact connected manifolds of
  non-negative curvature there is a constant $C(n)$ with $\sum_i b_i\leq C(n)$ (Betti
  numbers over any field); hence connected sums of sufficiently many copies of
  products $S^p\times S^{n-p}$ admit no non-negatively curved metric.

- **Finiteness and compactness (Cheeger [Cr 2]; Weinstein [We 1], Peters [Pe]; Gromov
  [Gv 1]).** For fixed dimension there are finitely many homeomorphism types among
  compact Riemannian manifolds with volume bounded below (or injectivity radius
  bounded below), diameter bounded above, and curvature bounded in absolute value; the
  set of such manifolds has compact closure in a suitable topology, with applications
  including Berger's $(\tfrac14-\varepsilon)$-theorem. See Pansu's Bourbaki seminar
  [Pa], the survey of Berger [Br 3] on the role of the Sphere Theorem, the references
  in Sakai [Sa], and another proof of the sphere theorem (after Gromov) in Eschenburg
  [E].
